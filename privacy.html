Privacy Policy

whit — log how you feel.

Last updated: 20.10.2026 · Deutsche Fassung

In short: whit works fully offline. Everything you write stays on your device unless you voluntarily sign in for cloud sync. There are no ads, no tracking, and nothing is ever sold or shared with third parties — not even in aggregated or anonymised form. Technical crash reports are off unless you switch them on yourself (section 4). The website counts visits without cookies and without storing IP addresses (section 11).

3. Cloud sync (optional)

You can create a free account in the settings to back up your data and use it on more than one device. Nothing is transmitted before you do. The provider is Google Firebase (Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore), operated by Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland, acting as a processor under Art. 28 GDPR.

Everything you sync is end-to-end encrypted. Your entries, notes, habits, name and settings are encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM) before they are uploaded, with a key that never leaves your devices unencrypted. Google stores only unreadable ciphertext, and neither Google nor the developer of whit can read your data — not in the database, not in the Firebase console. What remains visible is technical: your email address and account ID (needed to sign in), how many documents your account holds and when the server stored them. Document names are random and reveal nothing, not even when an entry was created.

The key is protected by a recovery code that whit shows you once when you set up sync. You need it to open your cloud copy on a new device. Nobody can restore it for you: if you lose it, the cloud copy can no longer be decrypted. The data on your device is not affected, and you can replace the cloud copy with it at any time.

In addition, your data is stored in an area scoped exclusively to your account identifier, and the database security rules permit access only to the signed-in account itself.

Transfers to third countries (in particular the USA) cannot be ruled out when using Google services. Google is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework; the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses apply in addition.

If you sign in with Google, Google learns that you are signing in to whit as part of that process. Google's privacy policy applies additionally.

4. Crash reports

To find and fix errors, whit uses Firebase Crashlytics (Google Ireland Limited, address as above) — only if you have switched crash reports on under Settings → Privacy → Crash reports. The switch is off by default. With it on, if the app crashes, a report is transmitted containing the technical cause: the error message, the position in the program code, the app version, the device model, the operating system version and a randomly generated, app-specific installation identifier.

The contents of your entries — mood values, notes, tags and habits — are never part of a crash report. The identifier is not linked to your account and is reset when the app is reinstalled.

Legal basis is your consent, Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, given by switching crash reports on. Google retains crash reports for 90 days.

You can withdraw your consent at any time by switching it off again. It takes effect immediately, crash reports not yet sent are deleted from the device, and the choice applies to this device only — it is not synced, so another device can neither switch it on nor off for you.

5. Legal bases

  • Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of a contract) — for storing your entries locally, without which the app would have no function.
  • Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (consent) — for cloud sync, which you give by voluntarily creating an account and can withdraw at any time by signing out or deleting the account.
  • Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR — information about your mood and wellbeing may constitute health data under Art. 9 GDPR. It is processed solely on the basis of your explicit consent, given by using the app and its optional cloud backup.
  • Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR (consent) — for crash reports, which you give by switching them on and can withdraw by switching them off (section 4).

6. Retention

Local data is kept until you delete it or uninstall the app. Cloud data is kept until you delete it or delete your account. There is no automatic retention period and nothing is retained after account deletion.

7. Deletion and your rights

In the app:

  • Individual entries — delete them in History; the deletion propagates to your other devices and to the cloud.
  • All data — Settings → Data → "clear all data". Deletes the data on the device and in the cloud.
  • Your account and all cloud data — Account sheet → "delete account". Permanently removes the account and every database record belonging to it.

Deletion without the app installed: email gobi275@proton.me from the address you registered with. The account and all associated data will be deleted within 30 days, usually much sooner. Every route in detail — including what is kept — is on the Delete account and data page.

You have the rights to access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction of processing (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20) and withdrawal of consent (Art. 7(3)) at any time. For portability, the app offers a direct JSON or CSV export under Settings → Data, so you do not need to contact anyone.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority.

8. App permissions

  • Notifications — only for the reminders you set yourself. The app works fully without this permission, just without reminders.
  • Vibration — haptic feedback while using the app.
  • Boot completed — restores your reminders after the device restarts.
  • Biometrics — only for the optional app lock. Your operating system performs the check entirely; the app receives nothing but "unlocked" or "not unlocked". whit never reads, stores or transmits fingerprint or face data — it never leaves your device's secure hardware.

The app requests no access to location, camera, microphone, contacts or files. When importing a backup you pick the file yourself through the system picker.

About the app lock, plainly: it guards access to the interface; it does not encrypt the database on the device. Anyone with physical access to an unlocked or rooted device could in principle read the files. Your operating system's own encryption applies to the device lock itself.

9. Minimum age

whit is intended for people aged 16 and over and does not knowingly collect data from anyone younger. The threshold follows Art. 8 GDPR: in Germany, a young person can only give valid consent to an online service processing their data from the age of 16. That consent is exactly what cloud sync and the processing of your mood entries as health data rest on (section 5).

Without an account the app processes everything locally on the device. If I learn that an account belongs to someone younger, I delete it along with all data attached to it.

11. This website

This section is about the website only, not the app. The website is served by GitHub Pages (GitHub Inc., USA). When you open it, GitHub processes the connection data it technically needs, such as your IP address, to deliver and protect the page; the GitHub Privacy Statement applies.

To understand how the website is read, it counts visits with GoatCounter (run by Martin Tournoij, Ireland; servers at Hetzner in Germany and Finland). No cookies are set and nothing is stored on your device. What is counted: the page, the referring page, browser, operating system, screen width, language and the country derived from the IP address, plus which parts of the page were reached and which buttons were pressed. The IP address itself is not stored; to avoid counting you twice, it is kept in memory together with the browser identifier for at most 8 hours. Only aggregated numbers are looked at; they cannot be traced back to you.

The legal basis is my legitimate interest in a website that is clear and works (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The links to Google Play carry a campaign tag (e.g. utm_source=website) so the Play Console can show how many installs came through the website; it contains nothing about you.
